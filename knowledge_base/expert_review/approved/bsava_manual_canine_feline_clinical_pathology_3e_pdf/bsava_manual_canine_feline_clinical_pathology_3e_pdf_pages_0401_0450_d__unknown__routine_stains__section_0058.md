# Routine stains

## Routine stains

The staining method used should guarantee rapid and good quality results to facilitate the visualization of struc­ tures by increasing the contrast between them. Several staining techniques have been used in cytopathology, although they can all be placed into two main categories:

- Romanowsky-based stains: these include MayGrünwald-Giemsa (MGG), Wright-Giemsa and Diff­Quik ® (which is a modified Wright-Giemsa stain). Samples are air­dried before staining. These stains adequately stain nuclei and they are superior for the evaluation of cytoplasmic details and extracellular material
- Trichrome Papanicolaou (Pap) and bichrome haematoxylin and eosin (H&amp;E) stains: these are based on wet­fixation by the use of a 95% ethanol solution. They are less commonly used in cytopathology, although they are considered to provide better nuclear and nucleolar detail (Figure 21.9).
