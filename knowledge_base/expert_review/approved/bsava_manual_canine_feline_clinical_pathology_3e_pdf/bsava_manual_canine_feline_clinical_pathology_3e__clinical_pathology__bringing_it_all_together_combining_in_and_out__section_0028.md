# Bringing it all together: combining 'in' and 'out'

## Bringing it all together: combining 'in' and 'out'

Reference laboratory testing and the in-clinic laboratory should be complementary, not competitive (Figure 1.8). For example, there are several testing options for feline leukaemia virus (FeLV; see Chapter 28), ranging from rela- tively cheap in-clinic immunoassays to more expensive reference laboratory testing. None of the options offers perfect sensitivity and specificity: false negatives and some false positives are inevitable. When testing for the virus in a population of cats with relatively few clinical signs, the prevalence of the virus will be very low, and consequently the predictive value of a positive test (PPV) will be poor.
