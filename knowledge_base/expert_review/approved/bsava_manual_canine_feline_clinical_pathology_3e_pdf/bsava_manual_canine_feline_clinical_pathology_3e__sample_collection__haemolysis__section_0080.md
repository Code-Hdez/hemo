# Haemolysis

## Haemolysis

Damage to the cells during or after sampling may lead to haemolysis (Figure 3.2a). This results in a falsely low RBC count and PCV (although the haemoglobin is not affected), and a falsely high MCHC and MCH. Causes of haemo lysis include:

- Narrow gauge needle
- Excessive suction on the syringe
- Excessive agitation of the blood in the tube
- Prolonged storage
- Storage at high temperatures.

Haemolysis can also occur in vivo in the intravascular form of immune-mediated haemolytic anaemia (IMHA) as well as with other causes of haemolytic anaemia such as oxidative injuries.
