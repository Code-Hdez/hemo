# Potassium

## Potassium

Hypokalaemia is relatively common in cats with azotaemic CKD. The reasons for this are unclear but likely to be multifactorial. Proposed mechanisms include inadequate dietary intake, increased renal losses and relative hyperaldosteronism. Hypokalaemia is uncommon in dogs with CKD unless they are receiving intravenous fluids without potassium supplementation. Hyperkalaemia is common in patients with AKI, particularly if they are anuric.
