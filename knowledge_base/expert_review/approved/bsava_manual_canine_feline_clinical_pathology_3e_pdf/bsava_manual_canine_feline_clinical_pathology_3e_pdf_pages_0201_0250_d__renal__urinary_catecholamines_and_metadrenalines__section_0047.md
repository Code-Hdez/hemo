# Urinary catecholamines and metadrenalines

## Urinary catecholamines and metadrenalines

These may be used in the diagnosis of phaeochromocytoma, although their use is hindered by the limited availability of testing and the lability of vasoactive amines and their metabolites. The urine must be collected into a plain tube, acidified, protected from light and shipped on ice to the reference laboratory, where it is analysed by high-pressure liquid chromatography with electrochem ical detection. The concentrations of the catecholamines and their metabolites are expressed as a ratio to the urin ary creatinine concentration, to take into account the spot nature of the sample. A recent study demonstrated that urine normetadrenaline:creatinine ratios were significantly higher in seven dogs with phaeochromocytoma than in controls, and that the performance characteristics of this metabolite were superior to those of adrenaline (epinephrine), noradrenaline (norepinephrine), dopamine and metadrenaline to creatinine ratios in distinguishing the affected dogs from healthy controls (Kook et al. , 2010).
