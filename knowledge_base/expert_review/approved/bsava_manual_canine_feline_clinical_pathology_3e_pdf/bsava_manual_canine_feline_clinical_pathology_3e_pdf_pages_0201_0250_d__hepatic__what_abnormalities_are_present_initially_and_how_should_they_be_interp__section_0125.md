# WHAT ABNORMALITIES ARE PRESENT INITIALLY AND HOW SHOULD THEY BE INTERPRETED?

## WHAT ABNORMALITIES ARE PRESENT INITIALLY AND HOW SHOULD THEY BE INTERPRETED?

The elevated total thyroxine (T4) result initially, in combination with the clinical signs the cat is exhibiting, secure a diagnosis of hyperthyroidism. Diabetes mellitus is an important differential diagnosis for weight loss in the face of a good appetite, especially with the reported polydipsia, but the hyperglycaemia is very mild and not accompanied by glucosuria so the cat is unlikely to be diabetic. Concern could be raised for intrinsic liver disease in view of the increased liver enzyme activity; however, this is most likely to be attributable to the hyperthyroidism.
