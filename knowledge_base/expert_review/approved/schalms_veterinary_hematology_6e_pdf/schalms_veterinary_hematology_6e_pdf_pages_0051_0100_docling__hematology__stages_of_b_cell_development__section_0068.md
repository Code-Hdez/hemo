# Stages of B Cell Development

## Stages of B Cell Development

The earliest defi ned cells of B lineage are pro - B cells. These cells have limited proliferative ability and are characterized by rearrangement of the Ig heavy chain. As maturation proceeds to pre - B cells, a cytoplasmic heavy chain protein is produced that combines with a surrogate light chain to form a transient pre - B cell receptor (BCR). Expression of the pre - BCR inhibits further heavy chain rearrangement, stimulates cell replication, and is necessary for the survival and positive selection of pre - B cells. Light chain rearrangement occurs in late pre - B cells. Kappa rearrangement proceeds fi rst. Lambda rearrangement will occur only if kappa rearrangement is unsuccessful. Light chain and heavy chain proteins combine to produce IgM. At this stage, cells also express cluster of differentiation (CD)10, CD19, and human lymphocyte antigen - DR (HLA - DR) markers.

As cytoplasmic IgM is exported to the surface membrane, cells transition into immature B - cells that express CD21. Surface IgM expression is used to delete cells reacting with self - antigen. Surviving immature B cells migrate to peripheral lymphoid tissues where they begin to express surface IgD as well as IgM. As these cells interact with antigen and T cells, they undergo the process of class switching, somatic hypermutation, and affi nity maturation.
