## Diagnostic Signifi cance

The antiglobulin test today is most commonly used in veterinary medicine in the diagnosis of IMHA. The use of the antiglobulin test as an aid in the diagnosis of IMHA in dogs was fi rst described by Miller et al. in 1954, and subsequently by Lewis et al. in 1963. 37,44 Direct antiglobulin test positive hemolytic anemia was fi rst described in cats in 1973 by Scott et al. 54 Several studies on the use of the antiglobulin test in detecting IMHA in the horse have been described. 38,40,43

Sensitivity of the antiglobulin test in detection of IMHA is moderately low, with sensitivity as low as 48% reported in dogs. 26 False negative test results may be secondary to factors such as incomplete washing of RBCs, insuffi cient RBC - bound antibody, elution of Ig or C3, or to a prozone effect. 48,63 Interestingly, positive results in the absence of IMHA can also occur. One study described a high incidence of C3b bound to RBC in dogs with infections, infl ammatory disorders, and myeloproliferative and lymphoproliferative diseases. 56 Hemolytic disease in these dogs was minimal or absent. The positive DAT was felt to be useful in dogs with signs of hemolytic disease, but needed to be interpreted with caution in other conditions. In another study using cats, 16 of 20 anemic cats were DAT positive. Eleven of the 16 were FeLV positive, and the remaining fi ve positive cats had infl ammatory disease or hemoplasmosis. Conservative interpretation of a positive test in the absence of hemolysis was recommended. 13
