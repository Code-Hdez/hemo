# Protein - losing Nephropathy

## Protein - losing Nephropathy

Nephrotic syndrome is a well - recognized hypercoagulable state, with reported systemic thrombosis rates of 40% in people. 59 Molecular markers of thrombin activation and platelet aggregation increase with even modest urinary protein loss. Hypoalbuminemia (i.e. serum albumin concentration below 2.0 g/dL) provides a surrogate measure for TE risk. 42 Protein - losing nephropathy is commonly associated with venous and arterial TE in dogs. 9,22 - 24,26,34,50,70 In one study, TE was documented in 26% of dogs with PLN that died or were euthanized; 9 however, relative TE risks have not been defi ned for specifi c renal disorders.

Urinary AT loss plays a major role in mediating the hypercoagulability of PLN. 23 Human patients experience thrombotic complications when plasma AT falls below 50 - 70%. 59 Concomitant hyperfi brinogenemia, hypercholesterolemia, high factor VIII, and hypofi brinolysis (due to high PAI - 1) are believed to contribute to TE risk. Hypoalbuminemia is associated with enhanced platelet aggregability in dogs and people, and platelet reactivity correlates with proteinuria in people. 23,59 Volume depletion, venous stasis, and immune complex activation may also promote TE secondary to PLN.
