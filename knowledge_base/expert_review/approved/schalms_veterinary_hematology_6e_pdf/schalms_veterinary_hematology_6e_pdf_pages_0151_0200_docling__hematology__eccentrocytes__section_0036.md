# Eccentrocytes

## Eccentrocytes

Eccentrocytes (also termed erythrocyte hemighosts) are RBCs with eccentrically shifted hemoglobin within the cell, leaving a pale, hemoglobin - free area. 18 These cells may progress to pyknocytes which are eccentrocytes in which the cell membrane has collapsed. Eccentrocytes have been reported to be easier to visualize on NMB stained preparations. 30 These cells are associated with oxidative damage to the erythrocyte membrane and cytoskeleton. 6 In horses, eccentrocytes have been observed with red maple toxicity ( Acer rubrum ), erythrocyte glucose - 6 - phosphate dehydrogenase defi ciency, and fl avin adenine dinucleotide defi ciency (see Chapter 36 ). 18,35 In dogs, eccentrocytes have been observed with ingestion of substances known to cause oxidative damage, such as onions, vitamin K, garlic and acetaminophen. Additionally, eccentrocytes have recently been described in dogs with diabetes mellitus, T cell lymphoma and severe infections. 6 Cattle receiving intravenous hydrogen peroxidase may have circulating eccentrocytes. 36
