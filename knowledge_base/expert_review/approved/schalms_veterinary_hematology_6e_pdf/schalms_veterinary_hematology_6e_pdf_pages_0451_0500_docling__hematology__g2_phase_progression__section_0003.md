# G2 PHASE PROGRESSION

## G2 PHASE PROGRESSION

T ransition from S to the G2 phase occurs upon completion of DNA replication. At this point, somatic cells are tetraploid (i.e. have four copies of DNA) and are destined to divide.


The S phase checkpoint is fallible, so a partially redundant checkpoint exists during the G2 phase. ATM/ATR kinases also act as G2 - DNA damage sensors. Lymphocytes that undergo physiologic DNA double strand breaks during antigen receptor gene rearrange- ment are particularly susceptible to chromosomal instability, which is why patients with ataxia telangiectasia have hypofunctional adaptive immune responses and a signifi cantly higher risk of developing cancer. 2 The primary G2 DNA damage effector kinase is Chk1, which controls G2 phase progression and M phase entry, primarily by phosphorylating the CDK - regulatory CDC25 phosphatases.
