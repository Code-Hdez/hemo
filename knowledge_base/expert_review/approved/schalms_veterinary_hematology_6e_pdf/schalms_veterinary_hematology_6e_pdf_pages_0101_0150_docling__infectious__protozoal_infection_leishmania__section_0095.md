# PROTOZOAL INFECTION - LEISHMANIA

## PROTOZOAL INFECTION - LEISHMANIA

Leishmaniasis is a disease caused by protozoal organisms of the genus Leishmania , and is transmitted in Mediterranean regions by the bite of an infected female sand fl y. The vector in North America is not known. Several forms of the disease, including cutaneous, mucocutaneous, and visceral forms, have been identifi ed in dogs. A mild anemia and thrombocytopenia are the most frequent hematologic fi ndings. Microscopic examination of fi ne needle aspirates of lymph nodes or bone marrow, along with serology and polymerase chain reaction (PCR), are frequently used diagnostic methods (Fig. 19.1 ). In one study, Leishmania spp. amastigotes were observed in the marrow of 92.6% of clinically affected dogs. 20 Severe histiocytosis of the marrow has been reported. 14
