# General clinical signs associated with thrombocytopenia

Animals with a low platelet count may have no visible clinical signs, and the
change may first be found on a routine complete blood count. When bleeding
signs occur, they may include petechiae in the skin or mucous membranes,
ecchymoses or bruising, nosebleeds, blood in feces or urine, prolonged bleeding
after venipuncture, and bleeding within the eye.

The presence and severity of bleeding cannot be determined from the platelet
count alone. Platelet function, vascular disease, other coagulation disorders,
the rest of the blood count, and the underlying disease process can all affect
the clinical picture. A low platelet count increases bleeding risk, but
hemorrhage is not present in every affected animal.
