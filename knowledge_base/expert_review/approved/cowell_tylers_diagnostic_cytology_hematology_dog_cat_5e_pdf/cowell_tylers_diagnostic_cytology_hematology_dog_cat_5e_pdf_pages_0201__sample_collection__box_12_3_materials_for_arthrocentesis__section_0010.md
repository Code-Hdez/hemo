# BOX 12.3 Materials for Arthrocentesis

## BOX 12.3 Materials for Arthrocentesis

- 1-inch, 25-gauge needles (small dogs and cats)
- ½-inch, 22-gauge needles (large to medium dogs)
- 3-mL syringes
- 3-mL ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) blood tubes (lavender top)
- 3-mL no additive blood tubes (red top)
- 20-mL blood culture bottle (for 1-3 mL of fluid)
- Glass slides
- Clippers
- Sterile gloves
- Sterile scrub solution and alcohol

Fig. 12.3 Arthrocentesis of the carpus joint. The joint may be located by applying fingertip pressure just distal to the radius during flexion and extension. The needle is introduced between the distal radius and proximal to the radial carpal bone. (From Piermattei DL, Flo G, DeCamp C. Chapter 1: Orthopedic examination and diagnostic tools. In: Brinker, Piermattei, and Flo's Handbook of Small Animal Orthopedics and Fracture Repair . 4th ed. St. Louis, MO: Saunders; 2006:24.)


The stifle is flexed, and the needle is introduced just lateral to the patellar ligament and distal to the patella. The needle is advanced in a medial and proximal direction pointing toward the medial condyle of the femur (Fig. 12.7). ␣


The femur is abducted and the leg extended caudally. The needle is introduced cranial to the greater trochanter of the femur and inserted caudal and distal or ventral toward the joint (Fig. 12.8). ␣

Fig. 12.4 Arthrocentesis of the elbow joint. With the elbow in hyperextension, the needle is introduced medial to the lateral epicondyle of the humerus and lateral to the olecranon. (From Piermattei DL, Flo G, DeCamp C. Chapter 1: Orthopedic examination and diagnostic tools. In: Brinker, Piermattei, and Flo's Handbook of Small Animal Orthopedics and Fracture Repair . 4th ed.
