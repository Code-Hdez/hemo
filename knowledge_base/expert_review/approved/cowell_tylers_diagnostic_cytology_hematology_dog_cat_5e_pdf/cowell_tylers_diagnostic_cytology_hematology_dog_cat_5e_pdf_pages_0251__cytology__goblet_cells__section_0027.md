# Goblet Cells

## Goblet Cells

Goblet cells (see Fig. 16.6) are mucus-producing bronchial cells that are generally elongated (i.e., columnar) with a basally placed nucleus and round granules of mucin, which frequently distend the cytoplasm. 39 Occasionally, the cytoplasm is so distended that the cell appears round. The granules stain from red to blue to clear with Romanowsky (e.g., Giemsa-Wright) stains. Free granules from ruptured goblet cells may be seen in the smear (see Fig. 16.6, B). The shape of the cells and the large size of the granules are helpful in differentiating these cells from mast cells (see Fig. 16.6, C). Goblet cells are not frequently seen; however, any chronic pulmonary irritant may result in increased numbers of goblet cells. ␣
