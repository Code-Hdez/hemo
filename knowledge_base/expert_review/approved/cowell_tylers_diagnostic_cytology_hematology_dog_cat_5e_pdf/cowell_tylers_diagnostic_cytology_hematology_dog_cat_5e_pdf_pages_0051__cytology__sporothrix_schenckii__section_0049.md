# Sporothrix schenckii

## Sporothrix schenckii

Infection by this agent may cause cutaneous, cutaneolymphatic, or disseminated lesions. In dogs, cutaneous lesions often occur on the head or trunk and typically present as nodules. These may ulcerate with purulent discharge and crusts. In cats, draining tracts are typically noted on distal limbs, head, or tail base. Samples from these sites typically reveal neutrophilic to mixed inflammation, with variable numbers of yeast organisms noted in the macrophages, in the neutrophils, and in the background. In particular, cats often will have large numbers of organisms in discharge material, and for this reason cats with this infection need to be handled with caution to prevent zoonotic spread. The yeast organisms are pleomorphic in shape, varying from round to oval to cigar shape, 2 to 10 μ m in length, and 1 to 3 μ m wide (Fig. 5.12). Although similar in size to Histoplasma , Sporothrix may be distinguished by its variety of shapes. ␣
