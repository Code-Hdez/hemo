# Myelofibrosis

## Myelofibrosis

Myelofibrosis is often accompanied by hematopoietic hypocellularity. Some retained foci of persistent hematopoiesis or compensatory hyperplasia of marrow elements commonly exist, along with increased hemosiderin stores (see Fig. 27.36). Myelofibrosis is rarely a primary form of myeloproliferative neoplasia and is much more commonly secondary (Box 27.14). In dogs, causes include immune-mediated disease, especially PIMA; neoplasia; inflammation, infection, vascular injury or necrosis in the marrow; drug-associated or with irradiation; pyruvate kinase (PK) deficiency; or an idiopathic change. 4,18,31,52 Myelofibrosis is less common in cats but can occur with immune-mediated anemia, FIP, chronic renal failure, or neoplasia, such as MDS/AML. 52 Myelofibrosis is most commonly accompanied by nonregenerative anemia with normal neutrophil and platelet counts, but neutropenia and/or thrombocytopenia can be seen in more severe cases. Ovalocytes and dacryocytes can be seen in circulation in some cases but are not specific for myelofibrosis. 27,53 ␣
