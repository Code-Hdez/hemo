# Trichoblastoma (Basal Cell Tumor)

## Trichoblastoma (Basal Cell Tumor)

These tumors were previously referred to as basal cell tumors in dogs and cats. These are neoplasms that are derived from primitive hair germ and thus are thought to actually be of follicular origin. 11 Basal cell carcinoma and other epitheliomas may have a similar cytological appearance, and definitive characterization of these tumors requires histopathology. Basal cell tumor continues to be a general diagnostic term to encompass these tumors for cytological diagnosis, although the majority of 'basal cell tumors' are trichoblastomas. These are common in both cats and dogs. The tumors are usually solitary, firm, alopecic nodules that are dome shaped to polypoid. Larger masses may be ulcerated. The appearance in cats is similar, although the tumors also are often pigmented and may have areas of central necrosis and cyst formation. Aspirates from these lesions tend to be moderately to highly cellular. They may have a thin background separating sheets and clusters of cells, particularly if accompanied by cyst formation (Fig. 5.36, A). The clusters often form palisades, fronds, or ribbons (see Fig. 5.36, B). The cells have small- to moderate-sized, round nuclei, with a small amount of blue cytoplasm. They are often densely packed together. A small amount of fibrillar pink material may sometimes be seen along the edge of clusters, which may be basement membrane. Scattered individualized cells may be noted along with a few thin spindle cells. Mitotic figures are usually not apparent. ␣
