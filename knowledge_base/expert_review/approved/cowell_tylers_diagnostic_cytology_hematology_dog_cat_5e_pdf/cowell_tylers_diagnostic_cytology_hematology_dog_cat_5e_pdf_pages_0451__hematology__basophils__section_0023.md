# Basophils

## Basophils

Basophils are the largest of the mature granulocytic cell types and rare in peripheral blood of healthy dogs and cats. Nuclei are less densely staining and have fewer lobulations and a more elongated, ribbonlike appearance than the nuclei of other granulocytic cell types (Fig. 26.16). Cytoplasm is moderately blue-gray to slightly purple and usually contains granules. In dogs, basophil granules are usually low in number and stain dark-blue to metachromatic. Canine basophils also occasionally lack obvious granules but are recognizable by their size, nuclear morphology, and cytoplasmic staining (Fig. 26.17). In cats, basophils contain abundant oval, pale-lavender to gray specific granules (Fig. 26.18), although immature basophils may also contain a few primary, dark purple granules. ␣
